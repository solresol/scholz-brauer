## The blocker and its resolution

The 4 October representation had sound abstract contexts, but its full local
list-based acceptance attempts timed out. This run preserves those contexts
and the accepted checkpoint. It replaces repeated list inclusion and edge
membership with exact natural-number bit sets. This capability directly removes
the active milestone's kernel-evaluation blocker; it is not a new search or a
new exclusion-certificate format.

`valueBits` maps a list to the union of singleton bits. `testBit_valueBits`
proves the exact membership equivalence, including lists with repetitions.
`mem_of_bits_subset` proves that bitwise intersection equality implies inclusion.
`checkBitContext_sound` proves exclusion by induction on the remaining budget.
Every state checks its own value encoding; every split checks its edge encoding.
Child encodings are justified by the acceptance hypothesis for those children.
A child cannot invent a retained value merely by supplying a forged mask.

The checker still verifies the endpoint envelope, all viable next sums, every
child endpoint and budget, retention of inherited summands and the newly added
value. Its gap rule checks the complementary summand by bit membership.
`ChainReach.enlarge`, the doubling bound, active-value filter and the existing
upstream-chain reachability bridge are reused. Abstract contexts need not be
valid chains. Induction decreases the budget, so no shared-node truth cache or
assumption of an acyclic Python table is needed.

The data elaborator emits candidate constructors and bit masks only. It neither
runs the proof checker nor constructs acceptance proofs. Every mask equality
and local obligation is evaluated by `decide +kernel`; the exported Python
metadata continues to say `lean_proof: false` until compilation is separately
established. No native proof acceptance is used.

## Bounded evaluation and reproducibility

Exact integers throughout; no random seed or heuristic search. Python 3.9.6,
Lean 4.27.0, mathlib `a3a10db0e9d66acbebf76c5e6a135066525ac900`; dependency
pins are unchanged. Probe output and the complete build log are retained in
`2026-10-05-bit-context-checks.json`. This is certificate validation rather than
a fresh exhaustive chain search.

The primitive probe checked 12,510-bit intersection and high-bit membership.
On contexts 7104–7135, with only their referenced children in a balanced lookup,
the old checker passed in 17.910176 seconds and the initial bit checker in
7.702352 seconds. Each had a 120-second limit and a 2048 MB Lean allocation cap;
they ran sequentially. This measures that sample, not a universal speed ratio.
The gap rule was subsequently reduced from pair enumeration to complement lookup.

The final checker passed full-table chunks
10, 11, 12, 13, 220, 221, 222, 500, 700, 900 and 933 in 11.247881 seconds,
within a 180-second/2048 MB limit. These include the highest estimated pair and
coverage costs, plus widely separated states. The full build used a 1200-second

The complete proof build passed in **262.909207 seconds**, including the final
optimality and Scholz theorems. This is a first build of the twelve new proof
modules, using already compiled dependencies and data; it is not a clean-room
build timing.

Initial proof drafting exposed simplifier recursion in an unrestricted `simp`;
an explicit `simp only` proof resolved it. One root-directory Lake invocation

was launched before export finished and correctly reported a missing file;

counted as a proof or a mathematical obstruction.
