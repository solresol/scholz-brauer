# Chain 9 of 5,784,689 has no labelling with n-1 doublings

19 October 2026, Monday, Australia/Sydney (AEDT). Milestone 3: the "next
frontier" of `TODO.md`. Milestones 1 and 2 are complete and were not reopened.

## Result

Clift lists 16 optimal 27-step chains for n = 5,784,689. They are saved in
`data/2026-10-18-clift5784689-chains.json`; the page hash is unchanged since
8 October. Chain 9 is

    1 2 4 8 16 32 64 65 97 128 225 353 706 ... 180736 180801 361537 723074
    1446148 2892296 2892393 5784689

and its parent graph is unique. It is the run-generalised Clift graph G(5,9,3)
defined below; Clift's selected 109 graph is G(1,0,0). A *labelling* gives each
of its 54 edges a nonnegative shift, with B1 = 1 and B_v = 2^a B_p + 2^b B_q.
Its cost is the sum, over tails, of the largest outgoing shift.

**No labelling of chain 9 with B_n = 2^n - 1 has cost n - 1 = 5,784,688.**
So chain 9 does not give l(2^5784689 - 1) <= 5,784,715 through a Clift
labelling. The smallest possible cost is therefore at least n; it was not
computed.

This is a **computational exclusion**. Two complete enumerations with sound
pruning, using different decompositions and written independently, both find
no labelling: `scripts/clift5784689_loss_search.cpp` (primary) and
`scripts/clift5784689_detour_search.cpp` (second program, validated against
brute force). It is not a Lean theorem. It says nothing about chains 1-8 and
10-16 (different graphs) or about other addition chains for 2^5784689 - 1. It
neither establishes nor refutes Scholz-Brauer at 5,784,689. Clift's statements
that 5,784,689 is the first non-Hansen number and that these 16 chains are its
optimal chains remain literature claims, not checked here. No novelty or
priority claim is made.

The search visited 10,771,735 nodes with the critical path through 4-8
(families A, B) and 10,161,180 nodes through 4-5-7-15 (family C). It used
635,246 job runs and about 6,400 core-seconds on an Apple M1 (2-4 workers).
No configuration survived.

## Reduction

1. *(Lean, `CliftLabelling.lean`)* B_n = 2^n - 1 forces n distinct path
   weights. Hence cost >= n - 1, and cost n - 1 forces the weights to be
   exactly 0..n-1. The path P* of weight n-1 uses its tail's cap on every edge,
   and every vertex off P* has all outgoing shifts 0.
2. A doubling vertex has two parallel out-edges. If it were off P*, both
   shifts would be 0 and two paths would have equal weight. So P* passes all
   three runs and is one of 12 paths: 4-8-15-23, 4-8-23 or 4-5-7-15-23
   (families A, B, C), then F_b-51 or F_b-28-51, then G_c-sink or G_c-58-sink.
3. Write loss = (n-1) - weight. Every path is P* with a set of
   non-overlapping detours. A detour leaves P* by a non-P* edge, follows
   zero-shift edges and rejoins. Every loss is a sum of positive detour losses,
   and the losses are exactly 0..n-1.
4. **Configuration.** The 22 doubling-type differences fall into classes:
   - U: the 5 differences of the run 1..32;
   - v: the difference at 32 -> 64;
   - W: w at 64 -> 128 and d3 = |λ+ν-ξ|;
   - F: the 9 run differences and d2;
   - G: the 3 run differences and d1.

   With SS for subset sums, the n losses are the disjoint union of nine
   components:

       box t+SS(all)       eK A1+SS(U,v,F,G)   ep A1+p+SS(U,v)
       eq  A1+q+SS(U,v,G)  tK A2+SS(U,F,G)     tp A2+p+SS(U)
       zK  A3+SS(F,G)      zp A3+p             zq A3+q+SS(G)

   Paths through 8 form the box. Paths through 4->5, 2->7 and 1->5 form the
   e, t and z parts. The sizes are 2^22, 2^20, 64, 1,024, 2^19, 32, 2^14, 1
   and 16, summing to n. All atoms are positive except the anchor: P* is the
   box minimum (t = 0, families A and B) or the eK minimum (A1 = 0, family C).
5. **Greedy enumeration.** Each component is down-closed: dropping a
   difference or the offset p or q gives another element. So the smallest loss
   not yet produced is the minimal element of a new atom. That atom is one of:
   - a base t, A1, A2 or A3 equal to x;
   - p or q, at x minus the smallest known base that contains it;
   - a difference, at x minus the smallest active base containing its class.

   The search branches on which atom it is and adds every new loss. A repeat
   or a loss >= n fails. Every anchored configuration is reached exactly once.
   Classes are *lazy*: a known difference carries the block of the class
   partition induced by the active components, and blocks are refined (with
   branching) when a component activates. So the class of a small difference
   such as 1, 2 or 4 is decided only when it first matters.

## Pruning, all necessary conditions

- **Exact cover.** Losses are stored as bitsets (5.78M bits), with
  word-parallel shifted ORs and an undo log.
- **Box bound.** t + (sum of all differences) <= n-1. Future differences are
  distinct and at least the current lower bound.
- **Halving.** Removing the components without class G (the 97 losses of the
  p-components) leaves a set halved by every G difference. So [0,n) has at
  most 97 odd runs in steps of that difference: 1,180 candidates. For F the
  exceptional set has 97 + 65*16 = 1,137 elements: 10,673 candidates.
- **Caps (critical-path zero constraints).** For each family, let P_k be the
  prefix cap sums along P*, with 0 = P_0 <= ... <= P_S = n-1. A detour from
  position s rejoining at e, with first shift in [0, c_s], has loss in
  [P_e - P_(s+1), P_e - P_s]. Detours sharing a first edge differ by exact
  P-differences, so for example p = P_r58 - P_r15 in families A and B. Run
  differences are at most their caps, d3 lies in [c_15, c_8 + c_15] (A),
  d3 <= c_8 (B) or d3 = c_15 (C), and similarly for d2 and d1.
  - Unknown atoms are replaced by valid lower bounds, using the current
    smallest uncovered loss.
  - Lazy classes are replaced by bounds valid for every resolution. For
    example, the run part of F is at least the sum of the b smallest possible
    members.
  - Feasibility is checked by Bellman-Ford on difference constraints, over
    the 8 (anchor t) or 4 (anchor A1) families and both roles of w and d3.
    Interior run positions carry only sums and are eliminated exactly.

## Validation (`results/2026-10-19-clift5784689-chain9.json`)

- **Loss decomposition and anchors.** 4,000 random labellings saturating a
  random critical path, over all 12 families on graphs up to n = 1,600 (seed
  20261019). The nine-component formula equals the explicit path-loss multiset
  every time. P* is the claimed anchor, and all atoms are nonnegative.
- **Caps soundness.** Every non-degenerate saturated labelling is accepted by
  the C++ caps test. Atoms are randomly hidden and replaced by valid lower
  bounds, and classes are widened to lazy blocks: 6,594 queries from 2,198
  non-degenerate labellings. A further 4,000 queries come from saturated
  labellings of G(5,9,3) itself and of G(5,5,3), G(3,9,1) and G(5,3,1), 1,000
  each, with atoms by longest-path dynamic programming. All are accepted.
- **Reference enumeration.** Without caps pruning, the C++ search returns
  exactly the configuration sets of the independent Python reference
  enumeration (`clift5784689_reference.py`) for G(1,0,0), G(2,0,0), G(1,1,0),
  G(1,0,1) and G(2,1,1), for both anchors. Each has 40 + 8 = 48
  configurations.
- **109 cross-check.** For G(1,0,0), these 48 are exactly the anchored,
  positive-atom configurations among the 216 interval configurations of the
  18 October box exclusion, which used a different method.
- **Leaf check.** Every such configuration is tested over every role choice
  and orientation by the independent generic check: particular labelling,
  exact interval test with big-integer bitsets, unique critical path, and
  Bellman-Ford on the cost-(n-1) system on the actual graph. None admits cost
  n-1.
- **Positive control.** Dropping groups of cap constraints (test hook)
  produces leaves that pass. For G(1,0,0), G(2,0,0), G(1,1,0) and G(1,0,1),
  both anchors and 8 drop masks (64 cases), the pruned search returns exactly
  the configurations that the same test accepts at the leaf: 419 in all, up to
  32 in one case. The intermediate relaxations therefore never discard a
  branch whose leaf passes.
- **Ladder.** The pruned search finds no configuration for G(a,b,c) at
  (1,0,0), (2,0,0), (1,1,0), (1,0,1), (2,1,1), (3,0,0), (5,0,0), (1,9,0),
  (1,0,3), (3,3,1), (5,1,1), (2,2,2), (5,3,1) and (3,5,2), for both anchors.
  Node counts (t + A1) are 34,327 at (1,0,0), 408,413 at (5,0,0), 182,047 at
  (1,9,0), 1,310,103 at (5,1,1), 4,286,111 at (5,3,1) and 20,932,915 at
  (5,9,3).
- **Job tree.** The chain-9 job tree is stored as every split record, with
  the totals of its directly run children. The checker re-derives the totals
  and confirms every split child is accounted for. It reproduces both root job
  lists by hash and reruns a seeded sample of jobs, which must give identical
  node counts.

## Independent second program

`scripts/clift5784689_detour_search.cpp` and `clift5784689_detour_check.py`
were written by a separate agent. It worked from a mathematical specification
only and never saw the primary program. Its evidence is
`results/2026-10-19-clift5784689-chain9-detour.json`. It shares only the facts
proved in Lean and the detour formulation, and works on the actual detours of
each critical path:
- it enumerates every candidate P* from the parent graph (12 for chain 9);
- it derives every detour and its compatibilities, and checks that their
  count gives n paths;
- it runs a minimal-excluded-loss search over individual detours, grouping
  interchangeable single alternatives into lazy blocks refined when a valued
  detour cuts them;
- it uses run and parallel-pair symmetry breaking, Bellman-Ford on the exact
  prefix-cap system of that P*, a look-ahead, a subset-sum packing bound and
  the halving parity test;
- at a leaf it solves the exact difference system and replays the explicit
  shifts.

Validation of the second program:
- it agrees exactly with a brute-force enumeration (no detour or
  critical-path theory) of all cost-(n-1) labellings: 1,552,956 labellings on
  all 472 parent graphs with n <= 14 and at most 6 steps, and 131,538 on 159
  graphs with n <= 18 and at most 5 steps, with 0 mismatches;
- every labelling it finds is replayed with big integers;
- switching its optional rules off changes nothing (an ablation over 1,066
  graphs);
- it finds no labelling for G(1,0,0), G(2,0,0), G(1,1,0), G(1,0,1), G(2,1,1),
  G(3,3,1), G(5,1,1), G(1,9,0), G(1,1,3) and G(5,9,0).

**For chain 9 it also finds no labelling.** All 12 critical paths were
searched exhaustively in 72 split jobs, between 03:33 and 05:25 AEDT on the
Mac (Apple clang 21) and raksasa (g++ 15.2). The search used 25,009,029
nodes and 29,397 CPU-seconds, with no leaf reached. Unsplit g++ runs of two
critical paths reproduced the summed split-job counts. Two jobs rerun on the
Mac gave identical counts, and the checker reruns one more.

Its own report notes a limit. Small chains almost never fail, so its negative
answers on nontrivial graphs rest on three things: the 109 agreement, the
ablation consistency and its documented soundness arguments. Its exact leaf
solver was exercised only on positive validation graphs.

Two different decompositions searched with independent code agree that chain
9 has no labelling of cost n-1. The primary program uses nine components with
a disjunction over critical-path families. The second program searches each
critical path separately over individual detours.

## Failed and bounded approaches

- **Unpruned greedy.** The greedy without cap pruning reproduces the 109
  results exactly. But its tree roughly doubles with each extra doubling step,
  from 0.6M nodes at G(1,0,0) to 6M at G(2,1,1). It was not used for chain 9.
- **Unsound first caps relaxation.** The first relaxation was unsound: its
  lower bound on the run part of F and G ignored unresolved candidate
  differences. The random saturated-labelling test caught it (31 rejections
  in 6,000 samples, all in family B), and it was fixed by counting every
  possible member. Exploratory ladder numbers from that build were discarded.
  All reported numbers come from the final program.
- **Forward checking.** Requiring every class with free capacity to have a
  feasible next shift pruned under 1% of nodes at seven times the cost. It was
  dropped.
- **Top-down halving.** A top-down halving search over the 97-element
  exceptional set, as first planned, was not needed. Halving survives as the
  candidate filter.
- **Parallel drivers.** The first parallel driver deadlocked on full pipe
  buffers and launched one process per job. The final driver
  (`clift5784689_parallel.py`) batches 64 jobs per process, reruns a slow
  batch job alone, and re-splits a single job that exceeds 300 s, three
  levels deeper. The "spine" job, a pure binary prefix, was re-split four
  times (t) and three times (A1). Batching changed only the driver; the search
  program is the one whose hash is recorded.
- **Observed pattern, not proved.** Every small G(a,b,c) enumerated without
  caps pruning had exactly 48 anchored configurations: 14 exploratory
  instances, five of them re-verified above. They look like the 109 ones with
  each run acting as binary digits at the bottom or the top. None passes the
  caps test even with the total cap budget relaxed by up to 1,000. No lifting
  lemma is proved.

## Next obligations

Apply the same method to the other 15 optimal chains. The eight variants of
the skeleton (chains 9-16) need their own component tables and cap
constraints; chains 1-8 are their transposes. A generic engine that derives
components and detours from the parent graph would avoid hand-derived tables.
To formalise the reduction in Lean, the remaining steps are the loss
decomposition, the minimal-element argument and the cap constraints.
