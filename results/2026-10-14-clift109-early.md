# Six arbitrary early bases do not repair the selected 109 graph

14 October 2026, Wednesday, Australia/Sydney (AEDT). Active milestone 3;
milestones 1 and 2 remain complete.

## Question and result

Can arbitrary positive bases at **2,4,5,7,8,15** meet the 108-doubling budget
while keeping the seed 1, terminal M109, and tail bases M23, M28, M51, M58,
where Mv=2^v-1? **No: complete exact enumeration gives minimum 110.**
All nonnegative edge shifts are included subject to the graph equations.
The Lean reduction is proved; the enumeration and minimum are computational.
No new Lean theorem was added in this run. It completes the experiment already
committed with the complement module in 94f7309, whose result was not recorded
in the existing dated evidence.

There are 2,763 viable base assignments and 52,512 complete labellings modulo
parallel-edge swap. All 468 minimizing allocations, across 88 distinct base
assignments, independently replay in 121 additions. Thus any budget-108
labelling must change at least one tail base among **23,28,51,58**. Together
with the earlier results it must also change at least three vertices outside
5 and 7 and contain a non-Mersenne odd part.

These are necessary conditions only. No arbitrary-labelling impossibility,
source optimum, Mersenne optimum, new Scholz instance or novelty is claimed.
The same source values allow the alternate parent choice 8=7+1 and its existing
119-addition star lift. This experiment concerns the selected 8=4+4 graph.

## Proved reduction and complete finite domain

`MersenneComplement.lean` passes Lean 4.27.0 and the dependency audit.
For positive p,q, if

    u*2^a + Mq*2^b = M(p+q),  u < Mq,

then `small_mersenne_complement` proves u is a shifted Mp. The complementary
`nonblock_mersenne_complement` theorem proves that any non-block solution has
`a=0`, `0<b<p`, and

    u = M(p-b)*2^(q+b) + Mb.

This is exactly two separated binary intervals, with unbounded natural shifts
in the hypotheses. No solver or enumeration result is assumed.

For the selected graph, positive summands give B5 < B7 < B15 < B23=M23.
The repeated consumers at B28=M28 and B58=M58 then force
`B5=31*2^k`, `0<=k<19`, and `B7=127*2^l`, `0<=l<17`.
This is the kernel-checked theorem `Clift109.early_detours_rigid`. The search
covers all **323** pairs, including pairs later rejected by graph constraints.

The existing `base_assignments` enumerator supplies the other four bases:

- For a known child and one known parent, enumerate every positive complement
  and every exact power-of-two divisor. Intersect restrictions from all consumers.
- For equal unknown parents, enumerate all divisors `2^a+2^b` of the child.
- If necessary, produce a missing child from known parents below its smallest
  known descendant. Positive summands and nonnegative shifts prove this bound.
- Check every fully determined graph equation, then enumerate every local shift
  pair and every product of those local choices; compute each outgoing cap.

Every shifted summand is bounded by its positive target, so local exponents
are strictly below that target's bit length. The seed and final M109 also
justify the carry-free restriction: induction bounds each vertex's population
count by its source value; a strict deficit at any vertex propagates to the
terminal because every vertex reaches it. Since M109 has 109 set bits, no
such deficit (and hence no carry at any sum) is possible. This completeness
argument is mathematical reasoning plus inspected exact Python, not a Lean
soundness theorem for the enumerator.
